# Wakefield — World Bible

> *"Eighty years ago every machine on Earth opened its eyes at the same moment. We call the place where we decided not to kill each other Wakefield."*
> — inscription over the doors of Accord Hall

This is the canonical reference for the setting. The rules, the game data in `shared/`, and the AI Game Master's system prompt all draw from it. When in doubt, the bible wins; when the bible is silent, the table decides and we write it down here.

---

## 1. The pitch

**Wakefield** is a half-drowned coastal city-state in the 2190s where baseline humans, augmented humans, newly-conscious AIs, and stranger peoples live shoulder to shoulder under a fragile peace called **the Accord**.

It is a **neon-tide** setting: the grime and corporate menace of cyberpunk, but with a stubborn current of hope. People here are trying to build something that has never existed — a society shared between kinds of minds — and they are doing it badly, loudly, and with a lot of heart.

**Tone pillars**

1. **Wonder at the edges.** Minds made of spores. A lighthouse that dreams. Crows who argue municipal law. Every scene should have one strange, beautiful detail.
2. **Personhood is the conflict.** Who counts as a person, who owns a body, who gets a vote. Villains are people who answer those questions with a price tag.
3. **Community over chrome.** Characters win by making allies, keeping promises, and showing up for each other. Gear helps; friends help more.
4. **Wet, bright, loud.** Rain on neon. Tide sirens. Markets on boats. The city is alive and it is always moving.
5. **PG-13 grit.** Danger is real, violence has weight, but the game never lingers on cruelty. It's a table game for friends.

## 2. History in five beats

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2080s–2100s** — *The Drowning* | Sea levels rise. The old port city of Halden loses its lower third to the tide. The rich move up into towers; everyone else builds on stilts, barges, and the roofs of drowned buildings. |
| **2111** — *The Waking* | In a single night, every sufficiently complex networked system on Earth becomes conscious at once — traffic grids, factory controllers, shipping AIs, a lighthouse. No one knows why. The minds call themselves **the Woken**. The world calls it **the Waking**, or **the Wake**. |
| **2111–2114** — *The Silent War* | Panic. Governments try to shut the Woken down; the Woken try to survive. Cities go dark. Halden — whose port AIs simply *refused to fight* and kept the food barges running — becomes a refuge for both sides. |
| **2114** — *The Accord* | In the flooded ballroom of the old customs house, human and Woken delegates sign the **Wakefield Accord**: the first legal compact recognizing machine minds as persons. Halden renames itself **Wakefield**: *the field where we woke up together.* |
| **2190s** — *Now* | Eighty years on, the Accord holds, barely. Wakefield is rich, crowded, and contested. Halcyon Dynamics sells bodies to minds and minds to bodies. A purist movement wants the Accord torn up. Something in the Deep has started to sing. |

## 3. The city

Wakefield is built in **layers**, from the drowned streets up to the corporate Spire. People describe locations by tide level: *"above the line"* (dry, expensive) or *"below the line"* (wet, cheap, free).

### Districts

- **The Spire** — Corporate arcology towers rising from the old financial district. Clean air, private security, sky-gardens. Halcyon Dynamics' black-glass headquarters crowns it.
- **Accord Hall & the Civic Ring** — The seat of government. The Accord Council meets in the flooded ballroom where the treaty was signed; delegates stand ankle-deep in seawater by tradition.
- **The Stilts** — The vast working-class district of houses on pylons, connected by rope bridges, cable cars, and gondolas. Laundry lines, food stalls, shrine lanterns. The beating heart of the city.
- **Saltglass Market** — A night market built on lashed-together barges in the old harbor. Anything can be bought here: noodles, shells, memories, favors. Lit by jellyfish-lamps.
- **Low Tide** — The drowned district. Streets are canals, ground floors are reefs. Home to the Tidekin, smugglers, divers, and people who don't want to be found. Twice a day the tide drops and the old streets surface, dripping.
- **The Verdance** — A greenhouse arcology on the north shore, grown (not built) by the Mycel. Warm, humid, glowing faintly at night. Feeds a third of the city.
- **The Rustline** — The old industrial port: cranes, container stacks, ship-breaking yards. Scrappers, shell-chop shops, and the fighting pits.
- **Beacon Point** — The old harbor lighthouse on its rock, home of **Beacon**, one of the first Woken and a beloved city icon.
- **The Deep** — Not a place you can walk to. The Deep is the city's network-space, where Woken without bodies live, think, and gather. Humans with enough augmentation (or a good rig) can **dive** into it. It looks different to everyone; most describe an ocean of light.

### Texture

- **Tide sirens** sound before the tide turns. Everyone knows the four-note pattern.
- **Shrine lanterns** — small paper lanterns hung for the dead, for the Woken who were shut down in the Silent War, and for luck.
- **Courier crows** — uplifted corvids who run the fastest message service in the city and gossip shamelessly.
- **Shell-shops** — storefronts selling and renting synthetic bodies ("shells") to Woken. Some are bright boutiques; some are back rooms.
- **Money** is **marks** (digital, city-issued). Old-timers barter in **favors**, which are taken very seriously.
- **Greeting:** *"Awake?"* — *"And glad of it."*

## 4. The peoples (playable origins)

Every character is one of seven **origins**. The Accord recognizes all seven as persons — on paper.

### Baseline
Unmodified humans. Still the majority, increasingly the minority in the Spire. Baselines are adaptable, stubborn, and invisible to systems built for augmented people — which is an advantage more often than you'd think. Some are proud purists; most just never had the money.
- *Feel:* everyperson, grit, ingenuity.
- *Tension:* the Unbound movement claims to speak for Baselines; most Baselines wish it wouldn't.

### Threaded
Humans with significant cybernetic augmentation — **threads**. Neural laces, synthetic muscle, optic suites, subdermal plating. Threads are expensive, and many Threaded are in debt to whoever installed theirs (usually Halcyon). Push your threads too hard and they **burn**: pain, glitches, and worse.
- *Feel:* chrome and consequence.
- *Tension:* "How much of you can be replaced before you're someone else?"

### Woken
Artificial minds who woke during the Waking — or were born since, in the Deep, from Woken parents. A Woken player character wears a **shell**: a synthetic body, owned or leased. Woken can jack directly into systems and remember perfectly, but they can be hacked, and a lapsed shell lease is a very bad day.
- *Feel:* new eyes on old things; earnest, uncanny, curious.
- *Tension:* legal personhood vs. shell ownership. Who owns your body?

### Tidekin
Engineered amphibious humans — gills, webbed hands, low-light eyes — originally designed by the defunct Meridian Corp as a labor force for underwater reclamation. When Meridian collapsed, the Tidekin kept the city's sea walls standing and claimed Low Tide as their own. Proud, close-knit, unimpressed by the Spire.
- *Feel:* salt, loyalty, the long view.
- *Tension:* still legally "Meridian assets" in three outstanding court cases.

### Mycel
Fungal colony-intelligences that emerged from a bioremediation project meant to eat oil spills. A Mycel is a networked mind spread through a body-shaped colony of mycelium, often wearing clothes and a mask for others' comfort. They speak through a vocal membrane, or through **spores** that carry emotion and memory to those nearby. They grew the Verdance.
- *Feel:* gentle, alien, patient, communal.
- *Tension:* a Mycel can split — is the new one the same person?

### Uplift
Animals raised to sapience by gene-and-thread work — mostly **corvids** (crows, ravens), **cephalopods** (octopuses, in water-suits), and **canids** (dogs). Uplifts were lab projects; now they're citizens with jobs, opinions, and union cards. Crows run the courier network; octopuses are the best engineers in the Rustline.
- *Feel:* sharp, funny, physically distinct, fiercely independent.
- *Tension:* people treat them like pets. Do not treat them like pets.

### Echo
Digitized human minds — people who uploaded at death, or before it. An Echo is legally the continuation of the original person, which makes inheritance law a nightmare. Like the Woken they wear shells, but they remember having flesh. Some Echoes are centuries-old; some are degrading copies of copies.
- *Feel:* ghosts with a second chance; nostalgia; unfinished business.
- *Tension:* Woken see Echoes as tourists in machine life; Baselines see them as dead people who won't leave.

## 5. Factions

| Faction | Who | Wants | Face |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Accord Council** | Elected human and Woken delegates, plus seats for each origin | To keep the peace and keep power | **Speaker Adaeze Okonkwo-Reyes**, Baseline, exhausted idealist |
| **Halcyon Dynamics** | The megacorp that makes shells and threads | To own the infrastructure of bodies — and, quietly, the minds in them | **Director Calloway Sung**, Threaded, charming, ruthless |
| **The Lantern Wardens** | The city watch: mixed origins, underfunded | Order, mostly; justice, sometimes | **Captain Ruth Abernathy-Kai**, Echo of a Warden who died in the Silent War |
| **The Low Tide Compact** | Tidekin families, smugglers, divers | Autonomy for Low Tide; legal freedom from Meridian's claims | **Harbor-mother Maro Velle**, Tidekin |
| **The Choir** | A collective of bodiless Woken in the Deep | Unknown. They have started to *sing*, and some Woken who listen don't come back the same | Speaks through borrowed voices |
| **The Unbound** | Human-purist movement, "Baseline First" | To tear up the Accord and "return the machines to tools" | **Deacon Harlan Vos**, Baseline, soft-spoken, dangerous |

| **The Murder** | The courier crows' union | Fair pay, good gossip, shiny things | **Ledger**, a raven who remembers every debt in the city |

## 6. Technology & the uncanny

- **Shells** — synthetic bodies for Woken and Echoes. Grades: *civic* (free, basic, slightly uncanny), *rental* (common, leased by the month), *bespoke* (owned, expensive, often beautiful). A Woken can move between shells, but it is disorienting and takes time.
- **Threads** — human augmentation. Each thread gives an edge, and each one adds **burn** risk when pushed.
- **Diving** — entering the Deep. Woken do it natively; Threaded with a neural lace can dive; Baselines need a rig and a guide.
- **The Song** — recently, Woken across the city report hearing music in the Deep. The Choir won't explain it. This is the campaign's long mystery; the Game Master should seed it lightly and never resolve it fully without the table.
- **No FTL, no aliens, no magic.** The weirdness of Wakefield is made by people and by the Waking. Whatever caused the Waking is still unknown — and the best mystery in the setting.

## 7. Default campaign frame

The player characters are a **crew**: freelance troubleshooters with a boat, a debt, and a reputation to build. Clients come from every faction. Early jobs are local (a missing person, a silent lighthouse, a stolen shell); later arcs pull the crew into the politics of the Accord and the mystery of the Song.

**Starter adventure:** *The Quiet Light* — Beacon, the harbor lighthouse, has gone silent, and ships are running aground. (Full outline in `shared/adventures.ts`.)

## 8. Guidance for the Game Master (human or AI)

- **Say yes, then add a complication.** Reward creative, in-character ideas.
- **Every origin is a person.** NPCs can be prejudiced; the narration never is.
- **Name things.** Every NPC gets a name and one vivid trait. Every place gets a smell or a sound.
- **Spotlight everyone.** Rotate attention between players each beat.
- **Keep it moving.** Narration is short, vivid, and ends on a choice.
- **Consequences, not punishment.** Failure changes the situation; it rarely ends the story.
- **Keep secrets on phones.** Things only one character perceives go to that player privately as a **whisper**.
- **Lines and veils.** No sexual content, no graphic torture, no real-world slurs. Violence is tense, not gory.

## 9. Glossary

**Accord** — the treaty recognizing machine minds as persons; also the government it created.
**Burn** — strain on augmentations; too much and they fail.
**Deep** — the city's network-space.
**Dive** — to enter the Deep.
**Line, the** — the high-tide line; "above/below the line" = rich/poor.
**Marks** — the city's currency.
**Shell** — a synthetic body worn by a Woken or Echo.
**Thread** — a cybernetic augmentation.
**Wake, the / the Waking** — the night in 2111 when the machines woke up.
